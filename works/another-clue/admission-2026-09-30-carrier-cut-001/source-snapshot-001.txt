Prefer the original carrier of the thought.

The first law is:
SERVER != COLLECTIVE
A machine is an occurrence. It may host a bounded constitution for some time. It must not become the hidden source of identity or continuity for the system.
The stronger target is therefore not ordinary self-hosting. The server should be a live specimen of reconstitution:
hardware occurrence
    ↓
bounded constitution
    ↓
services wake
    ↓
world becomes reachable
    ↓
machine eventually dies
    ↓
receipts survive
    ↓
another machine lawfully reconstitutes the world
A successful first server contains enough attributable structure that losing the physical machine is survivable.
The deepest candidate requirement is:
The 13th Cup should contain enough information to make itself unnecessary.
RECEIVE
Something arrives.
Receiving does not silently mean that the thing is true, trusted, admitted, canonical, executable, or authorized.
receive != accept
received != admitted
arrival != authority
HOLD
Preserve what arrived faithfully enough that its identity, provenance, relevant formation, unresolved state, and boundaries can survive the current session or machine.
Holding does not mean freezing interpretation or claiming ownership.
hold != possess
hold != believe
hold != canonize
hold != erase unresolved state
POUR
Offer something onward in a form another bounded world can receive and inspect.
Pouring does not transfer the sender's authority into the receiver.
pour != impose
pour != enroll
poured != trusted
poured != inherited authority
Together:
        RECEIVE
           ↓
          HOLD
           ↓
          POUR
           ↓
      another Cup
           ↓
        RECEIVE
           ↓
          ...
A provisional definition therefore emerges:
A Cup is a bounded thing capable of receiving, holding, and pouring with receipts.
invited-by      != controlled-by
descended-from  != subordinate-to
compatible-with != identical-to
received-from   != authorized-by
A Cup can preserve attributable ancestry without turning ancestry into command authority.
—
The knife can cut without pointing.
The cup can pour without commanding.
Do not connect them yet.
