The Infrastructure of Neighborliness

What if a neighborhood newspaper did more than report what happened? What if its stories also helped neighbors discover one careful, voluntary way to make something better next?

01 / What's growing?

A grocery that doesn't exist yet

Neighbors in Waseca are discussing possible community food arrangements. The reporting question is not simply where groceries are sold: it is what local ownership, shared responsibility, and accessible food could look like. Details and named accounts await checking and consent.

02 / People and places

The town is already connected

Rural communities have buildings, trades, traditions, tools and informal relationships. Which public resources can be connected without assuming that any person or organization has volunteered?

03 / The workbench

A small question worth answering

Could verified public information about food purchasing, printing costs, or a community meeting help someone decide a next step? This is a proposed reporting desk, not an open intake service or a request for personal histories.

04 / The penny page

Small things count

The printed paper is intended to be free to take. A separate optional real-penny bowl-and-box demonstration may help make the circulation of small acts tangible. There is no token, currency issue or verified coin inventory.
