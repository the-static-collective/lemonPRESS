# ONE TOWN. MANY CONGREGATIONS. ONE COMMON TABLE.
**lemonPRESS · The Open Page · Waseca · Independent community proposal · October 25, 2026**

*What if the churches helped their neighbors own a grocery store?*

A community-owned grocery is taking shape as a possibility in Waseca. Here's an invitation worth testing: What if congregations across town encouraged interested households to learn about the cooperative, consider becoming members through its **official** process, and help determine—alongside other owners—how it can serve the whole town?

The aim is **not** to establish a church-only store or give any congregation control over the town's groceries. It is to turn a shared religious calling to feed neighbors into practical cooperation, while preserving the cooperative's broader community ownership.

A small congregation may know how to organize volunteers. Another may have people who can help with rides, bookkeeping, meals, refrigeration, gardening or introductions. The food shelf has operational experience. Farmers and other residents have their own knowledge and questions. **What could they do together that none needs to promise alone?**

## A table, not a takeover

The experiment would invite churches to do three distinct things:

1. **Invite, don't enroll.** Invite interested members and neighbors to learn about the grocery cooperative. Only its verified official membership process can establish members, dues or voting eligibility.
2. **Serve together.** Offer a voluntary interchurch service table where people can coordinate useful skills, introductions and small, permission-based projects. Participation would not confer special governance authority.
3. **Bring back one result.** Try one concrete, consented food-access experiment, report what really happened and let the next decision follow the evidence.

Voting rights for individual and organizational cooperative members depend on the cooperative's bylaws and applicable law. Congregations cannot promise themselves votes, seats or operational control by joining this newspaper's invitation.

## The first practical question

What would it take to make everyday staples—milk among them—reliably affordable and accessible to more households?

A starting step is to ask local food-access workers what the actual problem is, whether they want collaborators, and what information can be shared publicly. We should not publish an unverified claim about a food shelf's grants, purchasing arrangements, shortages, or demand.

## Seven days to learn something real

**Day 1:** Invite one conversation at Real Life Covenant Church, without implying its endorsement or commitment.  
**Days 2–3:** Ask whether interested participants would each introduce up to two other congregations or community partners. An invited person may name an alternate representative instead.  
**Days 4–5:** Listen for one overlap of a confirmed need and a willing, capable helper. Seek the affected organization's permission before acting.  
**Days 6–7:** Attempt one small, safe action, or publish why no action was yet possible. Return a factual summary to everyone who opted in.

If someone declines, the invitation ends there. If a church, food shelf or cooperative organizer offers no commitment, we report none.
