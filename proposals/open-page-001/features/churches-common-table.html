A LITTLE NEWSPAPER FOR A COMMON TABLE

ONE TOWN.
MANY CONGREGATIONS.
ONE COMMON TABLE.

What if the churches helped their neighbors own a grocery store?

Waseca is exploring a community-owned grocery. Here's a question worth trying in public: What if congregations across town invited interested households to learn about the cooperative, consider becoming members through its official process, and work alongside other owners to make the store serve the whole town?

Not a church-only store. Not church control of the co-op. A way to turn the call to feed neighbors into shared practical work while leaving the cooperative open to the broader community.

One congregation has volunteers. Another has people who know transportation, bookkeeping, refrigeration, gardens or kitchens. Food-access workers, growers and other neighbors bring knowledge churches don't possess. What could they accomplish together?

A table, not a takeover

Invite, don't enroll. Interested people would join only through verified official cooperative channels. This newspaper neither sells membership nor collects dues.

Serve together. A voluntary interchurch service table could coordinate skills and small, permission-based projects without special governing authority.

Bring back one result. Test one real problem, document what happened, and follow the evidence. Cooperative votes and organizational membership depend on bylaws and applicable law; none are promised here.

The first useful question

What would it take to make everyday staples—milk among them—more reliably affordable and accessible? First ask local food-access workers what they actually need and whether they welcome collaborators. No unverified shortages, grant details or institutional commitments are being claimed.

Seven days to learn something

  1. Day 1: Invite an exploratory conversation at Real Life Covenant Church—not an announcement of its participation.
  2. Days 2–3: Invite up to two introductions per willing participant. Anyone unable to attend may suggest an alternate representative.
  3. Days 4–5: Find one overlap between a confirmed need and a willing helper. Obtain permission.
  4. Days 6–7: Try one small, safe action—or report candidly why it hasn't happened. Share a factual return with opt-in participants.
One town. Many congregations.
One common table.

This is an independent proposal from The Static Collective / Abundent, not an announcement by Real Life, another church, the Waseca Area Food Cooperative or a food shelf. No congregation, co-op organizer or food pantry has been enrolled or endorsed this proposal through this article.
