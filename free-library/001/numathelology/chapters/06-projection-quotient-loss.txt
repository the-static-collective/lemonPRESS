CHAPTER 6
PROJECTION, QUOTIENT, AND LOSS
6.1  Observation maps
Definition 6.1 (Observation map). An observation map O: W → Y sends a world to the information available under a declared cut or decoder. Two worlds are observationally equivalent when they have the same image under O.
An observation map can be mathematically exact and still insufficient for a later question. The distinction is between correctness of the summary and completeness relative to a target decision.
6.2  Same state, different world
Let G₁ be the six-cycle C₆ and G₂ the disjoint union C₃ ⊔ C₃ on six labeled vertices. Observe only the tuple consisting of vertex count, edge count, and sorted degree sequence.
O(C₆) = O(C₃ ⊔ C₃)
Lemma 6.2 (Re-expression cannot restore erased distinction). If O(W₁)=O(W₂), then for every deterministic function f defined on the observation space, f(O(W₁)) = f(O(W₂)). Therefore deterministic re-expression of O alone cannot identify which of W₁ or W₂ generated the observation.
Proof. Equality is preserved by functions. ∎
The minimum discriminator in this specimen is one additional Boolean graph predicate: connectivity. A failed candidate such as “all degrees even” remains true for both worlds and therefore adds no separating information.
DGRAM SSW-001 · EXACT FINITE WITNESS · SAME (V,E,DEGREES) · CONNECTIVITY SEPARATES
6.3  Target-relative sufficiency
Definition 6.3 (Target-relative sufficiency). An observation O is sufficient for target question Q on a class of worlds if any two worlds with the same observation induce the same correct answer to Q.
Sufficiency is therefore indexed by a target. The observation above is sufficient to answer “how many vertices?” and insufficient to answer “is the graph connected?” The same data product can be adequate and inadequate without contradiction.
Corollary 6.4. Adding computation without adding observation cannot repair an information loss that identifies worlds requiring different answers to the target question.
6.4  Quotient and section
Many useful numerical outputs are obtained by quotienting a richer object and then selecting a representative, coordinate, or section. Write [x]ᴳ for the equivalence class of x under grammar G and D_F for a decoder associated with frame F. A common pattern is
v = D_F([x]ᴳ).
The scalar v may be perfectly meaningful while failing to identify x. The route from x to v contains at least three independent design choices: carrier X, equivalence grammar G, and frame or section F. Changing any one can alter the interpretation without changing the printed numeral.
Definition 6.5 (Sectioned quotient mathal). A sectioned quotient mathal is a crossing X → [X]ᴳ → value_F in which both the quotient grammar and the final frame are addressable components of the receipt.
