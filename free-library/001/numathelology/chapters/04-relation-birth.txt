CHAPTER 4
RELATION BIRTH
4.1  Minimum arity
Some relations are impossible below a minimum number of participants. A binary relation requires at least two argument positions; a nondegenerate triangle requires three vertices; a tetrahedral 3-simplex requires four. The number at which a relation first becomes admissible is a structural fact about the declared relation family.
Definition 4.1 (Relation birthday). For a relation type ρ in a declared family W, its relation birthday is b_W(ρ) = min{n : ρ is admissible in Wₙ}, provided the minimum exists.
b_W(ρ) = min { n : ρ ∈ Adm(Wₙ) }
Remark 4.2. The birthday is a statement about admissibility or existence, not about discovery. If ρ is first noticed at m > b_W(ρ), the later observation does not backdate or redefine its minimum arity.
4.2  Birth certificates
Proposal 4.3 (Relation birth certificate). A birth certificate for ρ is a record B(ρ) = (S, b, σ, t, Π), where S is a participating set, b the established minimum arity, σ any orientation data earned by the relation, t the first attributable observed occurrence in the working corpus, and Π the positive formation receipt.
Only b is intended as an intrinsic numerical coordinate of the relation type. The remaining fields are specimen-level information. Keeping them separate prevents a later discovery event from being confused with mathematical birth.
Warning 4.4. A relation of arity k has no canonical projection to lower arity merely because all of its participants possess pairwise relations. In particular, ternary relation ≠ pairwise backprojection.
4.3  Pairwise surfaces can hide higher-order change
Consider two three-element vector matroids on the same labeled ground set {a,b,c}. In U₃,₃ every subset is independent. In U₂,₃ every singleton and every pair is independent, but the full triple is dependent. An observer restricted to singleton and pair queries cannot distinguish the two matroids.
Proposition 4.5. Pairwise agreement does not determine ternary dependence.
The proposition is elementary, but it is the cleanest finite witness for the rule GRAPH PROJECTION ≠ WHOLE. A graph records order-two adjacency. A hypergraph, matroid, simplicial complex, or other higher-order carrier may retain relations that disappear under pairwise projection.

4.4  Orientation is additional structure
Even when support and dependence agree, orientation may remain independent information. For an ordered basis in a real vector space, the sign of the determinant distinguishes two orientations while the underlying set of nonzero basis vectors remains the same.
Observation 4.6. Support does not determine orientation. Therefore a projection that retains only participating elements can erase an orientation distinction without erasing the relation itself.

Exercises

4.2. Give a ternary relation on three labels whose pairwise projections are all identical under two distinct ternary states.
