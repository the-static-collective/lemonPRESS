CHAPTER 3
NUMATHALS AND CARDINAL TRANSITION
3.1  Numerically indexed crossings
Definition 3.1 (Numathal). A numathal is a mathal for which a numerical parameter participates essentially in admissibility, typing, or consequence. A cardinal numathal is written Wₙ →ν Wₘ when the transition from n to m changes the available structure in the declared family.
The definition is deliberately stricter than “a mathal containing a number.” Multiplying a coordinate by five is not automatically a cardinal numathal. By contrast, passing from complete graphs on four vertices to complete graphs on five vertices is cardinal because the size of the vertex set changes the relation capacity and the relevant obstruction.
Definition 3.2 (Transition delta). For an invariant or property family I on worlds Wₙ, the transition delta at n→m is ΔI = I(Wₘ) − I(Wₙ) when subtraction is meaningful, and otherwise the ordered change record (I(Wₙ), I(Wₘ)).

3.3  Local transition grammar
Proposal 3.3 (Transition descriptor). For a family {Wₙ}, define a local transition descriptor Tₙ = (Aₙ, Oₙ, Lₙ, Dₙ, Πₙ), where A records newly admissible structures, O newly forced obstructions, L distinctions lost under the active decoder, D distinctions newly discriminable, and Π the receipts required to support those claims.
This descriptor is not intended to be canonical. Its role is to make a recurring question explicit: at n→n+1, which changes belong to the family itself, and which arise because the analyst changed the rules while moving the index?
Warning 3.4. A change in n and a change in world are independent coordinates. If both are altered simultaneously, the resulting effect cannot be attributed to cardinality without an additional control.
3.4  A five-field preview
Three classical facts will recur later. In simple planar graph theory K₄ is planar whereas K₅ is not. In affine geometry four points in R³ may be affinely independent whereas any five are dependent. In group theory S₄ is solvable whereas S₅ is not. Each transition occurs at five. The local mechanisms are different.
