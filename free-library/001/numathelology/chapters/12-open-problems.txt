CHAPTER 12
OPEN PROBLEMS
12.1  Existence of nontrivial whole-number grammars
The central open problem is classification: which transition types recur across mathematically unrelated families after local mechanisms are preserved and superficial numerical coincidence is controlled? A useful answer need not produce a universal law. Even a finite taxonomy of recurring transition mechanisms would be substantive.
Problem 12.1. Define an equivalence relation on cardinal transitions strong enough to identify genuinely shared structural mechanisms but weak enough to preserve local proofs.
12.2  Minimal mathal axioms
The present definition of mathal is intentionally permissive. A stricter theory might specify source and target categories, receipt functors, partial composition, refusal states, or equivalence lenses as first-class algebraic structure.
Problem 12.2. Find a minimal axiom system for receipt-bearing typed crossings that supports composition without collapsing endpoint, path, and trace equivalence. Determine when the resulting structure is already a known categorical or automata-theoretic object.
12.3  Birth and obstruction duality
Relation birth and obstruction threshold are superficially dual: one records the first index at which a structure becomes possible, the other the first at which a chosen representation becomes impossible. The duality is incomplete because admissibility and obstruction may live in different carriers.
Problem 12.3. Characterize families in which relation birth for a fine structure corresponds functorially to carrier obstruction in a coarse projection.
12.4  Receipt complexity
How much receipt is enough? Full path retention may be expensive; endpoint-only retention may be insufficient. Between them lies a compression problem: find the smallest trace sufficient for a specified class of future questions.
Problem 12.4. Given a class of targets Q, define and compute the minimum receipt sufficient to preserve all Q-relevant distinctions under composition.
12.5  Decoder lattices
Decoders can often be ordered by informativeness. A fine decoder refines a coarse one when its observation determines the coarse observation. This suggests a lattice or preorder of views over a fixed carrier.
Problem 12.5. Study threshold movement under decoder refinement: when does a relation birthday or obstruction index remain invariant as observation becomes finer?
12.6  Algorithmic discovery
Finite combinatorial databases permit systematic searches for abrupt changes in invariants across n. Such searches should treat pattern discovery and explanation separately. A machine can nominate a threshold; the local mathematics must still prove what changed.
Problem 12.6. Design a search procedure that proposes candidate numathals from finite data while emitting counterexamples, alternate decoders, and minimum-discriminator tests alongside each proposal.
12.7  Closing reference statement
The mature form of the subject will be measured by the quality of its refusals. It should refuse to identify equal outputs with equal histories, refuse to assign a cardinal transition to number alone when the world changed, refuse to treat absence under one decoder as nonexistence, and refuse to elevate a repeated pattern above the proofs that generated it. What survives those refusals is small enough to compute and large enough to compose.

CHAPTER B
COMPUTATION RECEIPTS
The following receipts collect the finite calculations used repeatedly in the text. They are given in compact form so that the prose chapters need not carry implementation detail.
B.1 Planar surplus. K₅ has C(5,2)=10 edges; simple planar maximum at n=5 is 3n−6=9; surplus=1.
B.2 Gaussian binomial comparison. For n=4 and q=3, numbers of k-dimensional subspaces of F₃⁴ are (1,40,130,40,1), versus subset counts (1,4,6,4,1).
B.3 Carrier matrix. A=[[1,1,0],[1,0,1],[0,1,1]] has det(A)=−2 over Z; rank 2 mod 2; det 1 mod 3; A⁶=I mod 3.
B.4 SSW graph specimen. C₆ and C₃⊔C₃ both have 6 vertices, 6 edges, and degree sequence (2,2,2,2,2,2); connectivity separates them.
B.5 Segmentation count. A six-position string has five inter-position gaps, each cut/not-cut; therefore 2⁵=32 contiguous segmentations.
B.6 Base-three representation. 022100₃ = 2·3⁴ + 2·3³ + 1·3² = 225.
B.7 Symmetric groups. S₄ is solvable; S₅ is not. The first alternating simple group A₅ occurs at order five.
B.8 Affine threshold. At most d+1 points can be affinely independent in Rᵈ; hence five points in R³ are dependent.

relation birthday. Minimum index at which a relation type becomes admissible in a declared family.
return mathal. A mathal whose endpoint equals its source while its receipt may remain nontrivial.
target-relative sufficiency. Adequacy of an observation for answering a specified target question.
whole-number grammar. Typed collection of structural transition statements across Wₙ→Wₙ₊₁ with local mechanisms and receipts.
