CHAPTER 1
DECLARED WORLDS AND MATHALS
1.1  Declared worlds
A mathematical object is never encountered without a surrounding choice of admissible structure. A set may be considered only as a set, as the vertex set of a graph, as a basis candidate in a vector space, as a collection of labeled positions, or as the support of a higher-order relation. The underlying tokens may be unchanged while the mathematical world changes.
For present purposes a declared world is a finite or finitely describable setting that states which objects, carriers, relations, and observation rules are in force. The declaration is not metaphysical. It is an accounting device that prevents a conclusion earned in one structure from being transferred silently to another.
Definition 1.1 (Declared world). A declared world W is a tuple W = (C, R, D, E), where C is a carrier class, R is the admitted relation or operation family, D is the active decoder or observation map, and E is the chosen equivalence discipline. Additional coordinates may be supplied when a problem requires lineage, orientation, boundary, or authority information.
The tuple is intentionally incomplete as a universal foundation. Its purpose is local sufficiency. The smallest world that can state and test the question is preferred to a larger world whose additional structure is unused.
Principle 1.2 (Smallest sufficient world). Declare enough structure to make the question well-typed, but do not load additional distinctions as if they had already become relevant.
1.2  The mathal
Many mathematical formalisms privilege objects and treat transformations as secondary. nuMathelology places a different pressure on the crossing itself. A transformation may carry information that is not recoverable from the endpoint. This becomes unavoidable whenever the same output can be reached by distinct routes.
Definition 1.3 (Mathal). A mathal is a typed, addressable mathematical relation or transformation whose source, target, admissibility conditions, and receipt semantics are declared. A mathal from A to B is written A →ᵐ B.
Definition 1.4 (Receipt). A receipt Π(m) is the declared information retained about the occurrence of m beyond its target value. Depending on the world, Π(m) may record a path, factorization, orientation, residual, refusal, provenance, or proof witness.
target(m₁) = target(m₂)  ⇏  m₁ = m₂
The failure of endpoint equality to determine the crossing is the first permanent distinction of the subject. It is not always relevant. If a declared question depends only on the endpoint, the extra path information may be safely quotiented away. The burden is to earn that forgetting rather than assume it.
Example 1.5 (Two routes to one endpoint). Let A, B, C be states and suppose m:A→C is direct while p:A→B and q:B→C compose to q∘p:A→C. If the world records only reachability, m and q∘p may be equivalent. If the world records path length, intermediate state, cost, or refusal history, they are distinguishable.
Remark 1.6. A mathal resembles a morphism, typed arrow, transition, rewrite, or labeled edge depending on the surrounding theory. The term does not identify these structures; it marks the crossing as an addressable object whose retained distinctions must be declared.
1.3  Working taxonomy
The taxonomy is functional rather than historical. A result need not use every layer. Ordinary graph theory remains graph theory; it enters nuMathelology when its structures are used to analyze a declared transition, relation birth, obstruction, or information-preserving crossing.
1.4  Equivalence lenses
Definition 1.7 (Equivalence lens). An equivalence lens E is a named rule that specifies which differences may be ignored for the current question. Two mathals may be endpoint-equivalent, path-equivalent, trace-equivalent, cost-equivalent, or equivalent under another declared lens without being identical.
same endpoint  ≠  same path  ≠  same trace
The insistence on a named lens is a practical defense against naked equality. If two bodies are called “the same,” the book asks immediately: the same under which projection, and for which question?

Exercises
1.1. Give two transformations on a finite set with the same target value but different receipts. State an equivalence lens under which they become equivalent.
1.2. Construct a declared world in which path information is irrelevant, then enlarge the question so that the same quotient becomes insufficient.
1.3. Compare the term mathal with a morphism in a category. List the extra axioms required before the two notions could be identified.
1.4. For a deterministic finite-state machine, propose a receipt that distinguishes two accepted strings ending in the same state.
