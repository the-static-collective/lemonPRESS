EDITORIAL NOTE TO THE SECOND EDITION


The First Edition introduced nuMathelology as a relation-first grammar for finite structural change. Its principal object was the mathal: a typed, addressable crossing whose source and target do not exhaust its mathematical identity when path, receipt, residual, orientation, refusal, or provenance remain relevant.


The First Edition was deliberately conservative about promotion. It distinguished THEOREM, COMPUTED, CONSTRUCTION, OBSERVATION, PROPOSAL, and INTERPRETIVE claims; insisted that equal endpoints do not imply equal crossings; and treated the shared index of unrelated thresholds as evidence for investigation rather than evidence for common cause.


The Second Edition preserves that structure and responds to a small but technically important ambiguity in the original treatment of relation birth.


The ambiguity is this:


    ARITY ≠ NUMBER OF DISTINCT PARTICIPANTS.


A binary relation is a subset of X². Its tuples have two coordinate positions. But a tuple (a,a) has only one distinct element in its support. Therefore a nonempty binary relation may exist on a one-element carrier. The sentence “a binary relation requires at least two participants,” if participant means distinct element, is false without an additional irreflexivity, off-diagonal, injectivity, or distinctness condition.


Likewise, a ternary tuple may have support size one, two, or three:


    (a,a,a)     has arity 3 and support size 1;
    (a,a,b)     has arity 3 and support size 2;
    (a,b,c)     has arity 3 and support size 3.


This distinction is elementary. It is also exactly the kind of distinction nuMathelology claims should not be silently collapsed. The appropriate response is therefore not a footnote. The appropriate response is a small bureaucracy.


The Second Edition introduces:
— tuple support;
— support size;
— diagonal strata;
— full-distinct loci;
— support spectra;
— arity-sensitive admissibility;
— distinct-support admissibility;
— three separate “birthday” coordinates;
— revised transition language for 1→2, 2→3, 3→4, and 4→5;
— a rule forbidding the phrase “requires k participants” unless participant semantics are declared;
— and a new named mathal, SUPPORT-SPLIT-001, whose sole purpose is to stop an otherwise harmless sentence from becoming mathematically false.


This is intentional overcorrection.


The method of the book is that small ambiguities are not repaired by rhetoric. They are repaired by declaring the missing coordinate.




PREFACE TO THE SECOND EDITION


The First Edition began from a practical difficulty: mathematical objects are routinely compared after information has been discarded. The Second Edition begins from a more embarrassing version of the same fact: mathematical prose is routinely compared after distinctions in the prose itself have been discarded.


“Two-place relation.”
“Relation involving two things.”
“Binary interaction.”
“Two participants.”


These phrases may coincide in an intended example. They are not definitions of the same object.


The distinction between arity and support is therefore not merely editorial. It is a specimen of the subject.


A k-ary relation has k coordinate places by type. A particular k-tuple may repeat values. The number of distinct values actually appearing in that tuple is its support size. If a theory requires all coordinates to be occupied by distinct elements, that requirement must be declared. If a graph-theoretic edge is modeled as an irreflexive pair, the exclusion of the diagonal must be declared. If a simplex requires distinct vertices, distinctness must be declared. If a symmetric relation is allowed to contain loops, that must be declared too.


The numerical threshold at which a structure first appears can therefore depend on which of these questions is being asked:


1. At what carrier size is the typed relation language meaningful?
2. At what carrier size can a nonempty relation of that arity exist?
3. At what carrier size can a witness with k distinct participating elements exist?
4. At what carrier size can the specific constrained relation type exist?
5. At what carrier size does the target invariant first change?
6. At what carrier size does the decoder first distinguish that change?


The same informal word “birth” can hide six different thresholds.


The purpose of the Second Edition is not to multiply terminology for sport. It is to make it possible to say exactly which threshold one means, then delete every coordinate that later proves unnecessary.


The book retains its original discipline:


    same endpoint ≠ same path ≠ same trace;
    same arity ≠ same support;
    same n ≠ same world;
    same observation ≠ same underlying structure;
    same threshold index ≠ same mechanism.


A useful formalism earns its complexity by making at least one otherwise-hidden distinction stateable, testable, and safely forgettable when it becomes irrelevant.

CHAPTER 1
DECLARED WORLDS, MATHALS, AND NON-COLLAPSE


1.1 Declared worlds


A mathematical object is never encountered without a surrounding choice of admissible structure. A set may be considered only as a set, as a graph vertex set, as a basis candidate, as the support of a tuple, as a labeled sequence, as a carrier for a k-ary relation, or as the state space of a transition system.


Definition 1.1 (Declared world).
A declared world is a tuple


    W = (C, R, D, E),


where C is a carrier class, R the admitted relation or operation family, D the active decoder or observation map, and E the chosen equivalence discipline. Additional coordinates may be supplied when a problem requires lineage, orientation, boundary, order, multiplicity, distinctness, authority, or receipt information.


The tuple is not proposed as a universal foundation. It is a local accounting device.


Principle 1.2 (Smallest sufficient world).
Declare enough structure to make the question well-typed, but do not load distinctions merely because they are available.


Corollary 1.3.
If a later counterexample depends on a distinction omitted from W, then either the original question did not require that distinction or the declared world was underspecified. The repair is to enlarge W, not to pretend the omitted coordinate had been present all along.


1.2 Mathals


Definition 1.4 (Mathal).
A mathal is a typed, addressable mathematical relation or transformation whose source, target, admissibility conditions, equivalence lens, and receipt semantics are declared to the extent required by the current question.


A mathal from A to B is written


    A →ᵐ B.


Definition 1.5 (Receipt).
A receipt Π(m) is the declared information retained about the occurrence of m beyond its target value.


A receipt may contain path, factorization, orientation, residual, refusal, provenance, multiplicity, proof witness, support profile, or another target-relevant discriminator.


Permanent distinction 1.6.


    target(m₁) = target(m₂)  ⇏  m₁ = m₂.


The reverse mistake also matters. Different receipts do not imply different target answers for every question. A receipt coordinate may be safely forgotten once target-relative sufficiency has been proved.


1.3 Non-collapse registers


The First Edition used non-collapse statements as a defensive grammar. The Second Edition expands the register:


    endpoint ≠ path;
    path ≠ trace;
    trace ≠ provenance;
    arity ≠ support size;
    coordinate multiplicity ≠ participant multiplicity;
    carrier size ≠ relation arity;
    typed possibility ≠ realized witness;
    witness existence ≠ target significance;
    observed absence ≠ mathematical nonexistence.


Definition 1.7 (Non-collapse register).
A non-collapse register is a finite list of distinctions that the current analysis refuses to identify without an explicit quotient or proof of irrelevance.


The register is not sacred. It is temporary scaffolding. Its purpose is to prevent accidental identification before the question determines which distinctions can be forgotten.


1.4 Equivalence lenses


Definition 1.8 (Equivalence lens).
An equivalence lens E specifies which differences may be ignored for the current target.


Two mathals may be endpoint-equivalent while not path-equivalent; path-equivalent while not provenance-equivalent; support-equivalent while not coordinate-equal; or observation-equivalent while not world-identical.


The question “are these the same?” is therefore malformed until the lens is supplied.

CHAPTER 2
MATHEMATICS BEFORE NUMBER


2.1 Distinction, relation, variation


Counting compresses. It reports how many under an individuation rule. It does not by itself determine which occurrences count as distinct, how they are related, whether repetitions are identified, which transformations are admissible, or which history produced the observed state.


The minimal pre-numerical kernel used in this volume therefore remains:


    DISTINGUISH
    RELATE
    VARY
    COMPOSE
    CONSTITUTE.


Write schematically:


    K = (D, R, V; ∘) —H→ ⟦·⟧ω.


Proposal 2.1 (Constitutive kernel).
D marks a distinction, R admits a typed relation, V proposes variation, ∘ composes compatible crossings, H is a constitutive crossing, and ω is retained trace.


The kernel is not intended as ontology. It is a bookkeeping grammar for refusing to identify proposal with adoption.


Construction 2.2 (Refused crossing).
Let a world have body C and lineage L. A proposal v is evaluated and refused. It may be that


    C′ = C,
    L′ = L,


while the broader event trace satisfies


    Λ′ ≠ Λ.


A world may therefore acquire history without acquiring the proposed body change.


2.2 Number as native dialect


Once a criterion of individuation is stable, counting becomes exact relative to that criterion.


Proposition 2.3.
If two worlds use different equivalence lenses for individuation, equal raw material may yield different counts.


Examples are standard:
— quotient sets count equivalence classes;
— connected components count under path connectivity;
— orbit counts depend on a group action;
— rank counts independent directions rather than listed vectors;
— tuple support counts distinct coordinate values rather than coordinate positions.


The last example is the Second Edition’s principal repair.


For x = (a,a,b),


    arity(x) = 3,
    support(x) = {a,b},
    support size = 2.


No contradiction is present because the two numbers answer different questions.


2.3 The three primitive pressure test


The relation-first slogan can be compressed further into three primitive capacities:


    FACT / DISTINCTION
    IDEA / VARIATION
    RELATION / TYPED CONNECTION


but this compression is interpretive rather than foundational.


A mathematically safer operational triad is:


    distinguish;
    vary;
    relate.


Why these three?


Without distinction, repeated occurrence cannot be typed as same or different.
Without variation, there is no transition grammar.
Without relation, there is no structured comparison among distinguished occurrences.


Composition is then an operation on crossings rather than a fourth ontological primitive.


Proposal 2.4 (DRV kernel).
A relation-first finite structural grammar may be generated from a distinction operator D, variation operator V, and relation constructor R, provided composition, typing, and constitution are supplied by the ambient world.


Ablation test:
— delete D: support and cardinality lose individuation;
— delete V: transitions become unstateable;
— delete R: structure collapses toward isolated labels.


The triad is therefore useful as a minimal research instrument. It is not claimed as a theorem about all foundations of mathematics.


2.4 Same numeral, different world


For subsets of a four-element set, ordinary binomial coefficients give


    (1,4,6,4,1).


For k-dimensional subspaces of F₃⁴, Gaussian binomial coefficients give


    (1,40,130,40,1).


The shared indices n=4 and k do not produce a shared structural interpretation.

2.5 Relation before object


A constituted relation may become an object at the next level of description. A path may be promoted to an edge in a path graph. A triangle may become a 2-cell. An equivalence class may become a quotient point.


Definition 2.5 (Relational promotion).
Relational promotion is the admissible use of a constituted relation ρ at level j as an addressable object at level j+1.


Promotion does not erase formation data unless an equivalence lens explicitly authorizes the quotient.

CHAPTER 3
NUMATHALS AND CARDINAL TRANSITION


3.1 Numathals


Definition 3.1 (Numathal).
A numathal is a mathal for which a numerical parameter participates essentially in admissibility, typing, consequence, capacity, obstruction, discriminability, or receipt.


A cardinal numathal may be written


    Wₙ →ν Wₘ.


Not every expression containing a number is a numathal in this sense. Multiplying a coordinate by five may be ordinary arithmetic. Passing from K₄ to K₅ changes the carrier size relevant to the complete-pair relation and its planar capacity.


3.2 Transition coordinates


The First Edition warned that n and world may change independently. The Second Edition makes that warning explicit.


Definition 3.2 (Transition coordinate vector).
For a comparison W→W′ define the bookkeeping vector


    χ = (Δn, ΔC, ΔR, ΔD, ΔE, ΔΠ),


where Δn records cardinal index change and the remaining entries record changes of carrier class, relation family, decoder, equivalence discipline, and receipt policy.


Principle 3.3 (Attribution discipline).
A consequence may be attributed to cardinality alone only when all non-cardinal coordinates relevant to the consequence are held fixed or their effects are separately controlled.


Thus:


    Δn ≠ 0 and ΔC ≠ 0


is not automatically a cardinal explanation.


3.3 Local transition descriptor


For a family {Wₙ}, define


    Tₙ = (Aₙ, Oₙ, Lₙ, Gₙ, Πₙ),


where:
Aₙ = newly admissible structures,
Oₙ = newly forced obstructions,
Lₙ = distinctions lost under the active decoder,
Gₙ = distinctions gained or newly discriminable,
Πₙ = receipts required to support the claim.


The change from the First Edition is only notational: G is used here for gained discrimination so D remains reserved for decoders.


3.4 Typed possibility versus full-distinct witness


A k-ary relation language can be written over a carrier of size one. A nonempty k-ary relation can also exist over a one-element carrier, namely by containing the repeated tuple


    (a,a,…,a).


Therefore the carrier threshold n=k is not a threshold for k-arity itself.


What does occur at n=k is the first possibility of a k-tuple with k distinct coordinates.


This distinction will govern the revised whole-number grammar.

CHAPTER 4
RELATION BIRTH, ARITY, AND DISTINCT SUPPORT


4.1 Arity


Definition 4.1 (k-ary relation).
Let X be a set. A k-ary relation on X is a subset


    R ⊆ X^k.


The number k counts coordinate positions.


It does not count distinct values occurring in a particular tuple.


Definition 4.2 (Tuple arity).
For x ∈ X^k, the arity of x relative to the ambient Cartesian power is k.


Arity is therefore a type-level coordinate.


4.2 Support


Definition 4.3 (Tuple support).
For x = (x₁,…,x_k) ∈ X^k, define


    supp(x) = {x_i : 1 ≤ i ≤ k}.


Definition 4.4 (Support size).
Define


    s(x) = |supp(x)|.


Then always


    1 ≤ s(x) ≤ k


for nonempty finite tuples.


Examples:


    x=(a,a)       gives k=2, s(x)=1.
    x=(a,b)       gives k=2, s(x)=2.
    x=(a,a,a)     gives k=3, s(x)=1.
    x=(a,a,b)     gives k=3, s(x)=2.
    x=(a,b,c)     gives k=3, s(x)=3.


Wolfram verification reproduces these support counts exactly by comparing tuple length with the length of its duplicate-deleted coordinate set.


4.3 Diagonal strata


Definition 4.5 (Full diagonal).
For X and arity k,


    Δ_X^k = {(x,…,x) ∈ X^k : x∈X}.


Every tuple in Δ_X^k has support size one.


Definition 4.6 (Full-distinct locus).
Define


    Dist_k(X) = {x∈X^k : s(x)=k}.


Equivalently, Dist_k(X) contains exactly the tuples with pairwise distinct coordinates.


Proposition 4.7.
Dist_k(X) is nonempty if and only if |X| ≥ k.


Proof.
If a tuple contains k distinct coordinates, X contains at least k elements. Conversely, if X contains k distinct elements, listing them gives a tuple in Dist_k(X). ∎


This proposition is the precise statement that informal language had previously compressed into “a k-ary relation needs k participants.”


It does not.


A k-ary relation needs k coordinate positions by type.
A full-distinct k-ary witness needs k distinct carrier elements.


4.4 Intermediate collision strata


The diagonal/full-distinct split is only the beginning. A k-tuple may have any support size from 1 through min(k,|X|).


Definition 4.8 (j-support stratum).
For 1 ≤ j ≤ k define


    S_{k,j}(X) = {x∈X^k : s(x)=j}.


Then


    X^k = ⨆_{j=1}^{min(k,|X|)} S_{k,j}(X),


a disjoint union by support size.


For k=3:
— S_{3,1} contains patterns of type aaa;
— S_{3,2} contains patterns of type aab, aba, baa and their value substitutions;
— S_{3,3} contains patterns of type abc with all entries distinct.


The partition is independent of any additional relation. A relation R⊆X^k may intersect some strata and omit others.


4.5 Support spectrum of a relation


Definition 4.9 (Support spectrum).
For R⊆X^k define


    Σ(R) = {s(x) : x∈R}.


When R is nonempty define


    μ(R) = min Σ(R),
    M(R) = max Σ(R).


For the empty relation, μ and M are left undefined unless a particular extended convention is declared.


Examples:
1. R = {(a,a)} has Σ(R)={1}.
2. R = {(a,b),(b,a)} on {a,b} has Σ(R)={2}.
3. R = X² for |X|≥2 has Σ(R)={1,2}.
4. Equality on X has support spectrum {1}.
5. Disequality on X with |X|≥2 has support spectrum {2}.


Thus two binary relations can have identical arity and disjoint support spectra.


4.6 Participant semantics


Definition 4.10 (Participant).
Unless otherwise stated in this edition, a participant in a witnessed relation instance is a distinct carrier element in the tuple support.


Therefore:
— (a,a) is a binary tuple with one participant;
— (a,b) is a binary tuple with two participants;
— (a,a,b) is a ternary tuple with two participants.


If an application wishes to count coordinate roles even when the same entity occupies multiple roles, it must say role-slot rather than participant.


Rule 4.11 (No naked participant count).
The phrase “a k-ary relation requires k participants” is forbidden unless one of the following is declared:
(a) all coordinates are constrained to be pairwise distinct;
(b) the relation is irreflexive/off-diagonal in the relevant sense;
(c) participant is explicitly redefined to mean coordinate role rather than distinct carrier element.


This rule is intentionally bureaucratic.


4.7 Three birthdays


The First Edition defined a relation birthday as the minimum n at which a relation type becomes admissible in Wₙ. The Second Edition splits this coordinate.


Definition 4.12 (Typed admissibility birthday).
For a relation schema ρ in a family W, let


    b_type(ρ) = min{n : the typed relation schema ρ is well-formed in Wₙ},


when such a minimum exists.


Definition 4.13 (Nonempty witness birthday).
Let


    b_wit(ρ) = min{n : Wₙ admits at least one witness of ρ}.


Definition 4.14 (Full-distinct witness birthday).
For k-ary ρ, let


    b_≠(ρ) = min{n : Wₙ admits a witness x∈ρ with s(x)=k}.


These may differ.


Example 4.15 (Unconstrained binary relation).
Over nonempty carriers:
— binary relation type is meaningful at n=1;
— a nonempty witness exists at n=1 via (a,a);
— a full-distinct binary witness first exists at n=2.


Thus


    b_type = 1,
    b_wit = 1,
    b_≠ = 2.


Example 4.16 (Disequality).
For ρ(x,y) iff x≠y:
— the schema is meaningful at n=1;
— no witness exists at n=1;
— the first witness exists at n=2 and is automatically full-distinct.


Thus


    b_type = 1,
    b_wit = 2,
    b_≠ = 2.


Example 4.17 (Nondegenerate triangle).
If a triangle is defined as three distinct vertices with all three pair edges present, then the distinctness constraint is constitutive. Its carrier birthday is 3.


This is not because “ternary” forces three distinct elements. It is because the declared triangle type does.


4.8 Birth certificates, revised


Definition 4.18 (Relation birth certificate).
A relation birth certificate is now


    B(ρ) = (k, S, b_type, b_wit, b_≠, σ, t, Π),


where:
k = arity,
S = support semantics,
b_type = typed admissibility birthday,
b_wit = nonempty witness birthday,
b_≠ = full-distinct witness birthday when meaningful,
σ = earned orientation data,
t = first attributable observed occurrence in the working corpus,
Π = formation receipt.


No one coordinate is silently substituted for another.


4.9 Pairwise surfaces and higher-order change


Consider U₃,₃ and U₂,₃ on the same labeled ground set {a,b,c}. Every singleton and every pair is independent in both. The full triple is independent in U₃,₃ and dependent in U₂,₃.


Proposition 4.19.
Agreement on all support sizes ≤2 does not determine a support-3 dependence relation.


This is a more precise form of the First Edition’s rule:


    GRAPH PROJECTION ≠ WHOLE.


A pairwise decoder can preserve every order-two observation while erasing a genuinely order-three distinction.

4.10 Orientation is additional structure


Support does not determine orientation.


Two ordered bases can contain the same underlying vectors and differ only in order, producing opposite determinant signs. Therefore:


    support equality ⇏ orientation equality.

CHAPTER 5
THRESHOLDS, OBSTRUCTIONS, AND CARRIER RUPTURE


5.1 Threshold language


Definition 5.1 (Threshold).
A threshold for property P in a family {Wₙ} is an index n₀ at which the truth, admissibility, necessity, obstruction, visibility, or support profile of P changes according to a declared rule.


A threshold must therefore specify at least:
— the family;
— the property or predicate;
— the indexing parameter;
— the comparison direction;
— and the decoder if visibility rather than existence is at issue.


Definition 5.2 (Monotone threshold).
A threshold is monotone when, after the first transition into the target predicate, subsequent indices remain in that predicate throughout the stated domain.


Definition 5.3 (Nonmonotone threshold event).
When a property can appear, disappear, and reappear, the book uses threshold event rather than implying one-way permanence.


5.2 Carrier rupture


Definition 5.4 (Carrier rupture).
A carrier rupture occurs at n₀ when preservation of a specified relation class across n₀ requires migration to a different admissible carrier or representation family.


The phrase “requires migration” is always relative to a preserved target. If the target is weakened, the rupture may disappear.


5.3 K₄→K₅ and planar capacity


For a simple planar graph with n≥3,


    |E| ≤ 3n−6.


The complete graph K₅ has


    C(5,2)=10


edges, while the simple planar bound at n=5 is


    3·5−6=9.


Therefore K₅ is nonplanar.


K₄, with six edges, is planar.


Proposition 5.5 (Five-field planar surplus).
Within the family of complete simple graphs under planar embedding, the complete pair relation at n=5 exceeds the planar edge-capacity bound by exactly one edge.


    10−9=1.


This is a local theorem. It does not license the sentence “five means obstruction.”


DGRAM F5-001 · VERIFIED

PLANAR CAPACITY 9
SURPLUS 1
K4 PLANAR
K5 NONPLANAR


5.4 Affine capacity


In Rᵈ, at most d+1 points can be affinely independent.


Thus in R³:
— four points may be affinely independent;
— every five-point set is affinely dependent.


Proposition 5.6.
Every five-point subset of R³ is affinely dependent.


Proof.
Choose one point as origin-relative anchor. The remaining four displacement vectors lie in a three-dimensional vector space and are linearly dependent. The corresponding affine dependence follows. ∎


Again, the threshold index is five. The mechanism is dimension, not planarity.


5.5 Symmetric-group solvability


S₄ is solvable.
S₅ is not solvable.


The relevant algebraic mechanism is the appearance of A₅ as a nonabelian simple composition factor in S₅.


The Second Edition retains this specimen because it is useful precisely when handled carefully: the same index hosts a third structural transition generated by a different proof.


Wolfram|Alpha independently confirms:
— symmetric group of degree 4: solvable = True;
— symmetric group of degree 5: solvable = False.


Methodological rule 5.7.


    COINCIDENT INDEX ≠ COMMON MECHANISM.


A higher-level transition class may be proposed only after the local mechanisms remain individually visible in the comparison.


5.6 The anti-numerology clause


The First Edition described repeated numerical coincidence as a research smell. The Second Edition formalizes the refusal.


Definition 5.8 (Index coincidence class).
For a fixed integer n, an index coincidence class is a collection of transition events whose declared thresholds occur at n.


Definition 5.9 (Mechanism-preserving comparison).
A comparison among events in an index coincidence class is mechanism-preserving when each event retains:
— its source family;
— target predicate;
— local proof or computation;
— relevant carrier;
— and required decoder.


Proposal 5.10 (Common-transition hypothesis).
A common-transition hypothesis may be stated only as a proposed relation among mechanism-preserving event records. The shared numeral alone is never the comparison map.


Thus the proper object is not


    five → mystical common cause,


but something closer to


    {(family_i, predicate_i, mechanism_i, threshold=5)}_i


followed by a search for a higher-order invariant that survives hostile controls.


5.7 Controls at five


A valid control should include families in which 4→5 produces no analogous qualitative transition.


Examples can be constructed by:
— taking a property constant in n;
— choosing a complete graph property that holds for both K₄ and K₅;
— choosing a vector-space statement whose threshold lies elsewhere;
— or selecting an invariant explicitly insensitive to the 4→5 change.


The presence of negative controls is not decorative. Without them, a catalog of “things that happen at five” cannot distinguish a genuine mechanism class from selection bias.


Exercises

5.3 State the affine dependence threshold as a function of d.
5.4 Explain exactly why K₅ nonplanarity does not imply S₅ nonsolvability.
5.5 Design an index coincidence class at n=3 and include at least one hostile control.

CHAPTER 6
PROJECTION, QUOTIENT, AND LOSS


6.1 Observation maps


Definition 6.1 (Observation map).
An observation map


    O: W→Y


sends a richer world to information available under a declared observation cut.


Two worlds W₁,W₂ are observationally equivalent under O when


    O(W₁)=O(W₂).


Observation equality is not world equality.


6.2 Same state, different world


Let


    G₁=C₆
    G₂=C₃ ⊔ C₃.


Observe only:
— vertex count;
— edge count;
— sorted degree sequence.


Then both yield


    |V|=6,
    |E|=6,
    degrees=(2,2,2,2,2,2).


But:
— C₆ has one connected component;
— C₃⊔C₃ has two.


Lemma 6.2 (Re-expression cannot restore erased distinction).
If O(W₁)=O(W₂), then every deterministic f on the observation space satisfies


    f(O(W₁))=f(O(W₂)).


Therefore deterministic re-expression of O alone cannot identify which world generated the observation when the target answer differs between them.


Proof.
Functions preserve equality. ∎


This lemma is intentionally obvious. Obvious statements become useful when they block sophisticated nonsense.


DGRAM SSW-001 · EXACT FINITE WITNESS
SAME V,E,DEGREES
DIFFERENT CONNECTIVITY


6.3 Target-relative sufficiency


Definition 6.3 (Target-relative sufficiency).
Observation O is sufficient for target question Q over class C when every pair W₁,W₂∈C satisfying O(W₁)=O(W₂) also induces the same correct answer to Q.


The same observation can therefore be:
— sufficient for “how many vertices?”;
— insufficient for “is the graph connected?”


Corollary 6.4.
Additional computation without additional discriminating information cannot repair an information loss that merges worlds requiring different target answers.


6.4 Support projection as a new specimen


The Second Edition adds a relation-theoretic version of the same-state problem.


Define


    P(x₁,…,x_k)=supp(x₁,…,x_k).


Then:
    P(a,b,a)={a,b}
    P(a,a,b)={a,b}
    P(b,a,a)={a,b}.


A support decoder forgets coordinate order and multiplicity pattern.


Thus equal support does not determine:
— coordinate order;
— equality pattern;
— orientation;
— argument role;
— or the original tuple.


Definition 6.5 (Support quotient).
The support quotient identifies tuples having the same set of distinct coordinates.


This quotient may be sufficient for questions about “which entities participated?” and insufficient for questions about “which role did each entity occupy?”


The relation arity/support repair is therefore another instance of the book’s original thesis:
a correct projection can be too coarse for a later target.


6.5 Equality-pattern decoder


A less coarse decoder records the partition of coordinate positions induced by equality.


For x=(a,a,b,c,b), the equality pattern is


    {{1,2},{3,5},{4}}.


This pattern records multiplicity structure without retaining the actual labels a,b,c.


Definition 6.6 (Equality-pattern decoder).
E_k(x) is the set partition of {1,…,k} in which i and j lie in the same block iff x_i=x_j.


Then:
— arity is recoverable from the domain size k;
— support size is recoverable as the number of blocks;
— labels are not recoverable;
— coordinate-equality structure is retained.


This gives a useful hierarchy of projections:


    tuple
      ↓ forget labels, keep equality pattern
    partition of coordinate positions
      ↓ keep only block count
    support size
      ↓ possibly threshold further
    Boolean full-distinct/not-full-distinct.


Each downward move can be exact and lossy simultaneously.


6.6 Quotient and section


Many numerical outputs arise from quotienting a richer object and then selecting a representative or coordinate:


    x ↦ [x]^G ↦ D_F([x]^G).


Definition 6.7 (Sectioned quotient mathal).
A sectioned quotient mathal is a crossing in which carrier X, quotient grammar G, and final frame or section F remain addressable receipt coordinates.


The scalar output may be valid while failing to identify the source.


6.7 Loss receipts


Definition 6.8 (Loss receipt).
A loss receipt records which distinctions are known to be erased by a projection.


For the support decoder:
— erased: order, role assignment, multiplicity positions;
— retained: set of distinct coordinate values.


For support size:
— erased: labels, order, role, exact equality partition;
— retained: number of distinct coordinate values.


A projection should therefore be documented not only by what it computes, but also by the distinctions it intentionally destroys.


Exercises
6.1 Prove the same-state lemma for arbitrary deterministic f.
6.2 Give two tuples with same support but different equality pattern.
6.3 Give two tuples with same equality pattern but different support labels.
6.4 Give a target question for which support is sufficient and one for which it is insufficient.
6.5 Construct the projection chain from a 5-tuple to its full-distinct Boolean.

CHAPTER 7
PATH, RETURN, AND RECEIPT


7.1 Closed endpoints


Returning to the same endpoint does not imply that nothing happened.


Examples:
— nontrivial loops in topology;
— holonomy after parallel transport;
— pure braids returning endpoints to their original positions;
— finite-state cycles with nonzero traversal count;
— reversible computations returning state while consuming path time.


Definition 7.1 (Return mathal).
A return mathal is m:A→A.


It is receipt-trivial only when the active equivalence lens also identifies Π(m) with the identity receipt.


    endpoint closes ⇏ history trivial.


7.2 Pure braid witness


The braid group B₃ maps onto S₃ by forgetting crossing history and retaining endpoint permutation.


    π:B₃→S₃.


The kernel is the pure braid group P₃.


A nonidentity pure braid may return every strand to its original endpoint while retaining nontrivial braid structure.


Proposition 7.2.
There exist mathals whose endpoint permutation is identity and whose braid receipt is nonidentity.


This is not metaphor. It is a standard quotient example of lost path information.


7.3 Support return


The Second Edition adds a simpler combinatorial analog.


Consider an ordered tuple evolving through coordinate permutations:


    (a,b,c)
       →
    (b,a,c)
       →
    (a,b,c).


The endpoint tuple returns exactly. A path receipt recording the intermediate transposition remains nontrivial.


Under the support decoder, every state along the path has the same support {a,b,c}. The entire loop becomes invisible.


Thus three levels coexist:
— exact state returns;
— support observation never changed;
— path receipt changed.


This specimen shows that observation constancy and endpoint closure are independent reasons history may disappear.


7.4 Residuals


Definition 7.3 (Loop residual).
Given m:A→A and decoder D with identical start/end observation, a loop residual is any declared invariant of Π(m) that remains nontrivial after endpoint closure.


Residuals need not matter forever. They are retained until irrelevance is proved relative to a target.


Principle 7.4 (Deferred discriminator).
When currently equivalent worlds retain distinct receipts, preserve the distinction until:
(a) target-irrelevance is proved; or
(b) a later target activates a discriminator.


7.5 Minimum receipt


Full path retention may be excessive.


Definition 7.5 (Target-sufficient receipt).
A receipt schema Π_Q is target-sufficient for question class Q when any two crossings sharing source, target, and Π_Q produce the same correct answer to every q∈Q.


Problem:
Find the smallest Π_Q under a declared receipt cost model.


This converts “keep the history” from a slogan into a compression problem.


Exercises
7.1 Give a return mathal invisible under support but visible under order.
7.2 Describe the quotient B₃→S₃ as retained and erased information.
7.3 Design a receipt sufficient to answer parity-of-permutation but not exact-path questions.
7.4 Give a case where full path retention is provably unnecessary.

CHAPTER 8
DECODER DEPENDENCE AND CARRIER CHANGE


8.1 Carrier, grammar, frame


A printed value is often downstream of several structural choices.


The book continues to use the X–G–F socket:


    X = carrier
    G = equivalence grammar
    F = frame or section.


A decoder state is written


    (X,G,F,D).


Equal outputs under different decoder states are not presumed comparable until a translation is supplied.


8.2 Scalar-carrier specimen


Let


    A = [[1,1,0],
         [1,0,1],
         [0,1,1]].


Over Z:
    det(A) = −2,
    rank(A)=3,
    A is invertible over Q and R.


Over F₂:
    det(A)=0,
    rank(A)=2,
    A is singular.


Over F₃:
    det(A)=1,
    rank(A)=3,
    A is invertible,
    A⁶=I.


The visible integer entries are unchanged. The carrier changes the algebraic consequences.


Independent Wolfram computation confirms:
— determinant −2 over Z;
— rank 2 mod 2;
— determinant 1 mod 3;
— sixth power equals identity mod 3.

8.3 Variation cubes


When three coordinates X,G,F each have two controlled settings, the factorial design is Q₃:


    {X₀,X₁}×{G₀,G₁}×{F₀,F₁}.


It has:
— 8 vertices;
— 12 edges;
— 6 square faces;
— one three-way interaction term under the usual contrast view.


A state cube records which coordinates differ.
It does not record the order in which changes occurred.


If order matters, path receipts are required.


8.4 Carrier ≠ decoder


Principle 8.4.
The carrier determines which structures can exist.
The decoder determines which existing distinctions are visible.


Changing a decoder cannot make an impossible carrier relation exist.
Changing a carrier can alter what a fixed-form decoder means.


Failure to observe a relation can therefore arise from:
— nonexistence;
— decoder blindness;
— quotient loss;
— insufficient sample;
— support collapse;
— or wrong carrier.


These are not interchangeable diagnoses.


8.5 Arity under carrier restriction


Suppose a k-ary relation R⊆X^k is moved to a smaller carrier Y⊂X.


The type k survives.
The admissible support spectrum may shrink.


For example, if |Y|=2 and k=3:
— ternary tuples still exist;
— support sizes 1 and 2 exist;
— support size 3 is impossible.


Thus carrier reduction may destroy full-distinct witnesses without destroying the k-ary relation language.


This is a canonical Second Edition example:


    CARRIER CAPACITY CAN CHANGE SUPPORT SPECTRUM WITHOUT CHANGING ARITY.


Exercises

8.3 Explain why changing |X| from 3 to 2 can destroy Dist_3(X) while leaving X³ nonempty.

CHAPTER 9
THE GRAMMAR OF THE WHOLE-NUMBER WORLD, REVISED


9.1 Grammar, not assignment


A whole-number grammar is not a dictionary in which every integer receives a metaphysical meaning.


Definition 9.1 (Whole-number grammar).
For a family {Wₙ}, a whole-number grammar is a typed collection of transition statements concerning Wₙ→Wₙ₊₁ together with:
— local mechanism;
— proof or computation;
— active decoder;
— support semantics where relevant;
— comparison lens;
— receipt;
— hostile controls.


The same transition name may occur at multiple n.
The same n may host multiple unrelated transitions.


9.2 The corrected low-cardinality corridor


The First Edition used the corridor 1→2→3→4→5 as a laboratory. The Second Edition preserves it and replaces every potentially sloppy universal statement with a typed one.


9.2.1 The 1→2 transition


Unsafe slogan:
“Relation begins at two.”


Corrected statement:
Carrier size two is the first size at which a tuple can contain two distinct carrier elements.


For binary arity:
— X² exists already when |X|=1;
— the diagonal tuple (a,a) exists at n=1;
— off-diagonal tuples first exist at n=2.


Therefore 1→2 can legitimately mark:
— birth of full-distinct binary witnesses;
— birth of disequality witnesses;
— birth of simple loopless graph edges between distinct vertices;
— birth of nontrivial two-element permutations;
— first possibility of a distinction involving two simultaneously present carrier elements.


It does not mark the birth of binary arity itself.


Candidate reading:


    1→2 = DISTINCT-PAIR ADMISSIBILITY


not


    1→2 = RELATION ITSELF.


DGRAM CORRIDOR-12
TYPE ALREADY POSSIBLE
OFF-DIAGONAL SUPPORT BECOMES POSSIBLE


9.2.2 The 2→3 transition


Unsafe slogan:
“Ternary relation begins at three.”


Corrected statement:
Carrier size three is the first size permitting a full-distinct ternary tuple.


Ternary tuples exist on carriers of size one and two:
    (a,a,a),
    (a,a,b),
    and so on.


What appears at n=3 is support stratum S_{3,3}.


Consequences in selected declared worlds:
— a nondegenerate triangle on three distinct vertices becomes possible;
— a three-way all-distinct interaction becomes representable without reusing participants;
— rank/dependence phenomena involving three distinct labeled elements can first be witnessed;
— oriented triples with three distinct elements become available.


But again:
    ternary type ≠ three distinct participants.


Candidate reading:


    2→3 = FULL-DISTINCT TERNARY ADMISSIBILITY


within worlds where that support distinction matters.


DGRAM CORRIDOR-23
TERNARY ARITY ≠ THREE-DISTINCT SUPPORT


9.2.3 The 3→4 transition


The transition 3→4 is especially tempting to narrate as “closure into volume” because four distinct points can support a tetrahedral 3-simplex.


That statement is valid in the simplex family:


A nondegenerate d-simplex requires d+1 affinely independent vertices.
Therefore a tetrahedron requires four distinct vertices.


Other structures also become possible at four, but no universal four-law follows.


K₄ is notable because:
— as an abstract graph it contains all six pair relations among four vertices;
— it remains planar;
— it is also the 1-skeleton of a tetrahedron, which is naturally realized in three dimensions.


This produces the First Edition’s “hidden lift” intuition:
a fine carrier can acquire higher-dimensional realization while a chosen coarse pair-relation projection remains planar.


Definition 9.2 (Hidden lift).
A hidden lift occurs when fine structure changes into a richer class while the active coarse projection remains in its previous observational class.


K₄ is a compact specimen, not a universal theorem about four.


Candidate reading:


    3→4 = SELECTED CLOSURE / HIGHER-CELL ADMISSIBILITY


with carrier and decoder declared.


9.2.4 The 4→5 transition


At five, several independent classical thresholds occur.


Graph world:
    K₄ planar;
    K₅ nonplanar.


Affine R³ world:
    four points may be affinely independent;
    five are necessarily dependent.


Symmetric-group world:
    S₄ solvable;
    S₅ not solvable.


The shared index is exact.
The shared mechanism is not established.


The proper grammar record is therefore:


    n = 5
    events = {planar-capacity obstruction,
              affine-rank obstruction,
              solvability transition}
    mechanism relation = UNKNOWN / PROPOSED ONLY IF EXPLICITLY SUPPORTED.


Candidate reading:


    4→5 = MULTIPLE INDEPENDENT CAPACITY/OBSTRUCTION EVENTS IN SELECTED FAMILIES.


This is intentionally less poetic and more useful.


9.3 The support ladder


The Second Edition introduces a general cardinal fact that is genuinely shared across all finite carriers:


Proposition 9.3.
For fixed tuple arity k, the maximum possible support size on an n-element carrier is


    min(k,n).


Proof.
A tuple has at most k coordinate values and cannot use more distinct values than exist in the carrier. Both bounds are simultaneously attainable by choosing min(k,n) distinct values and repeating as needed. ∎


Therefore as n increases from 1 to k, a new maximum support stratum becomes possible at every step:


    n=1: support ≤1
    n=2: support ≤2
    ...
    n=k: support ≤k.


This is a true general ladder, but its meaning is narrow:
it concerns distinct support capacity of k-tuples.


It does not imply:
— a universal geometric dimension;
— a universal algebraic threshold;
— a universal physical phase;
— or a universal metaphysical interpretation.


The support ladder is valuable precisely because its scope is explicit.


9.4 Representation and segmentation


The printed string


    022100


can be interpreted in base three:


    022100₃ = 225₁₀.


The same six visible positions, treated as a linear sequence with five inter-position gaps, admit


    2⁵=32


contiguous segmentations.


The printed carrier is unchanged.
The grammar changes.
The resulting mathematical object changes.


DGRAM REP-022100 · COMPUTED
BASE-3 VALUE 225
CONTIGUOUS SEGMENTATIONS 32


This specimen remains one of the book’s cleanest demonstrations that visible sameness does not imply structural sameness.


9.5 Revised candidate transition table


1→2
Candidate reading: first support-2 witness.
Required discipline: do not call this birth of binary arity.


2→3
Candidate reading: first support-3 witness; selected nondegenerate ternary structures.
Required discipline: separate ternary slots from three distinct participants.


3→4
Candidate reading: selected higher-cell closure or hidden lift.
Required discipline: name the family; no universal closure law.


4→5
Candidate reading: selected capacity failures and obstructions.
Required discipline: prove each mechanism locally; include controls.


n→n+1
Candidate reading: family-dependent structural transition.
Required discipline: report world, decoder, support semantics, proof, and receipt.


9.6 A rule for future integer grammars


Rule 9.4 (Cardinality claim checklist).
Before stating “at n, X becomes possible,” answer:


1. Is n carrier size, dimension, rank, order, arity, support size, or another parameter?
2. Is X a type, witness, full-distinct witness, invariant change, obstruction, or decoder event?
3. What coordinates are held fixed?
4. What is the local proof?
5. What adjacent n values were checked?
6. What hostile control fails to show the event?
7. What information is erased by the chosen observation?
8. Could the same sentence remain true if the carrier changed but n did not?
9. Could the same n occur without the event in another family?
10. What is the smallest surviving claim after all answers are supplied?


Only then may the integer be allowed back into the sentence.

CHAPTER 10
EXPERIMENTAL nuMATHELOLOGY


10.1 Status discipline


The status marks of the First Edition are retained.


THEOREM
Established from stated hypotheses by proof.


COMPUTED
Finite specimen reproduced by explicit calculation.


CONSTRUCTION
Explicit object, witness, or counterexample.


OBSERVATION
Pattern visible in the declared corpus or specimen set.


PROPOSAL
Well-typed reusable definition or grammar introduced for testing.


INTERPRETIVE
Cross-domain analogy used only to generate questions.


Second Edition rule:
A sentence may not borrow the evidentiary status of a nearby sentence merely because they are rhetorically continuous.


10.2 Hostile controls


A proposed grammar becomes more credible when obvious alternatives are given a chance to destroy it.


Basic hostile controls include:
— alternate carriers;
— alternate decoders;
— nearby cardinalities;
— diagonal versus off-diagonal witnesses;
— repeated-coordinate versus full-distinct tuples;
— randomized labels;
— null models;
— ablation of proposed primitives;
— counterexamples;
— minimum-discriminator searches;
— and support-preserving permutations.


Protocol 10.1 (Threshold test, revised).


For a candidate transition at n:


1. State the declared family.
2. State exactly what n measures.
3. State whether the claim concerns type, witness, full-distinct witness, invariant, obstruction, or observation.
4. Prove or compute the local change.
5. Vary the decoder while holding the world fixed.
6. Vary the world while holding n fixed.
7. Test adjacent indices.
8. Test diagonal and collision strata if relation arity is involved.
9. Search for a counterexample to the proposed higher-level grammar.
10. Report the smallest surviving claim.


The purpose is compression under attack.


A result that shrinks from a universal slogan to a precise local theorem has improved.


10.3 Minimum discriminators


Definition 10.2 (Minimum discriminator cost).
Given observationally equivalent worlds requiring different target answers, the minimum discriminator cost is the least additional observation budget sufficient to separate the relevant equivalence classes under a declared cost model.


For C₆ versus C₃⊔C₃ under the observation tuple (|V|,|E|,degree sequence), connectivity is a one-query discriminator when Boolean predicates each cost one.


For binary tuples under the decoder “arity only,” the query “support size?” distinguishes (a,a) from (a,b).


For ternary tuples under the decoder “support size only,” an equality-pattern query can distinguish (a,a,b) from (a,b,a) only if coordinate positions matter to the target.


There is no target-independent minimum discriminator.


10.4 Ablation


Definitions earn their place by surviving deletion tests.


Ablate receipt:
path-sensitive claims weaken or disappear.


Ablate decoder:
observation-relative sufficiency becomes ambiguous.


Ablate carrier:
existence claims become untyped.


Ablate support:
the distinction between (a,a) and (a,b) is unavailable when only arity remains.


Ablate arity:
support size alone cannot distinguish a binary off-diagonal pair from a ternary tuple with one repeated coordinate.


Principle 10.3 (Ablative minimality).
A primitive earns its place when deleting it makes at least one intended distinction unstatable or false.


10.5 Research receipts


Definition 10.4 (nuMathelological research receipt).
A research receipt for a transition claim contains:


— world declaration;
— parameter and numerical index;
— carrier;
— relation family;
— arity if relevant;
— support semantics if relevant;
— decoder;
— equivalence lens;
— local mechanism;
— proof or computation reference;
— failed controls;
— surviving controls;
— retained uncertainty;
— exact promotion scope.


Optional fields may be omitted only when demonstrably irrelevant.


10.6 The embarrassment protocol


The Second Edition adds a methodological rule for corrections.


Protocol 10.5 (Embarrassment protocol).
When a counterexample reveals that a phrase in the framework was underdeclared:


1. Preserve the original wording in the edition history.
2. State the smallest counterexample.
3. Identify the missing coordinate.
4. Split the ambiguous term rather than defensively redefining the counterexample away.
5. Re-run affected claims.
6. Promote only the repaired version.
7. Record whether the repair strengthens, weakens, or merely scopes the original claim.


The arity/support correction follows this protocol exactly.


Counterexample:
Binary tuple (a,a).


Missing coordinate:
Distinct support.


Repair:
Separate arity from participant count.


Effect:
The general methodology is strengthened; one informal low-cardinality slogan is narrowed.


10.7 Play and promotion


The experimental posture remains:


    PLAY FREELY.
    PROMOTE CAREFULLY.
    KEEP THE RECEIPT.


The first clause protects discovery.
The second protects mathematics.
The third protects the possibility of revisiting the decision.


DGRAM METHOD-002 · SECOND EDITION
PLAY FREELY
PROMOTE CAREFULLY
DECLARE SUPPORT
KEEP THE RECEIPT

Given observation O, HOLD-FIBER-001 retains distinctions among elements of a fiber O⁻¹(y) until target-relative sufficiency has been established.


Operational instruction:
Do not quotient away a difference before proving it safe to forget.


11.4 DEFERRED-DISCRIMINATOR-001


Definition.
When two worlds are observationally equivalent but retain distinguishable receipts, record the smallest known future observation that could separate them, or UNKNOWN.


11.5 RELATION-BIRTH-001, revised


Definition.
RELATION-BIRTH-001 maps a relation type to:


    (arity,
     support semantics,
     typed admissibility birthday,
     witness birthday,
     full-distinct witness birthday,
     orientation signature,
     formation receipt).


The First Edition’s single birthday coordinate is retained only as an abbreviation when these values coincide or when the omitted distinctions are provably irrelevant.


11.6 SUPPORT-SPLIT-001


Definition.
Given a tuple or relation statement in which arity and participant count have been conflated, SUPPORT-SPLIT-001 replaces the single quantity with:


    (k, s),


where k is arity and s is distinct support size.


For relation-level analysis, it may lift to:


    (k, Σ(R)).


Examples:
    (a,a)   ↦ (2,1)
    (a,b)   ↦ (2,2)
    (a,a,b) ↦ (3,2)
    (a,b,c) ↦ (3,3)


Use:
Whenever natural language says “k things are related” and it is unclear whether repeated occupation of coordinate roles is allowed.


Status:
DEFINITION / SECOND-EDITION REPAIR.


11.7 DIAGONAL-CHECK-001


Definition.
DIAGONAL-CHECK-001 asks whether a claimed minimum carrier size still holds when repeated-coordinate tuples are admitted.


Procedure:
1. Take the claimed relation arity k.
2. Evaluate the claim on the full diagonal Δ_X^k.
3. Evaluate it on intermediate support strata.
4. Evaluate it on Dist_k(X).
5. Report which stratum actually carries the claimed threshold.


This mathal exists because many incorrect “k participants” statements fail at step 2.


11.8 SUPPORT-SPECTRUM-001


Definition.
For relation R⊆X^k, return


    Σ(R)={s(x):x∈R}.


Optional receipt fields:
— multiplicity of each support size;
— equality-pattern classes;
— orbit counts under coordinate permutation;
— full-distinct proportion.


Use:
When a relation’s qualitative behavior depends on whether repeated participants are allowed.


11.9 CROSSING-SHEAR-001


Proposal.
A crossing shear is a discrepancy produced when two paths that agree under one projection separate under another.


The shear is indexed by:
— path family;
— coarse decoder;
— fine decoder.


A crossing shear is not promoted without an explicit witness.


11.10 CARRIER-RUPTURE-001


Definition.
Records a threshold at which a target relation class can no longer be preserved in the current carrier under the declared constraint.


Principal specimen:
K₅ relative to simple planar embedding.


11.11 SAME-STATE-DIFFERENT-WORLD-001


Definition.
A pair of distinct worlds with equal active observation and a target question that separates them.


Principal specimen:
C₆ versus C₃⊔C₃ under the tuple (|V|,|E|,sorted degrees).


11.12 ORIENTATION-SIGNATURE-001


Definition.
The minimum retained datum required to distinguish otherwise support-identical oriented structures under the chosen world.


Canonical example:
determinant sign for ordered bases.


11.13 FORMATION-MATHAL-10→01→2


The token


    10→01→2


is retained as a mnemonic formation mathal rather than an arithmetic identity.


Intended reading:
multiplicity → distinction → typed relation.


Second Edition clarification:
The terminal “2” is not a theorem that every relation begins with two distinct participants. It is a mnemonic for the first explicit relational contrast in that particular formation story.


Status:
PROPOSAL / MNEMONIC FORMALISM.


11.14 ARITY-SUPPORT-LIFT-001


Definition.
Given fixed k and carrier sizes n<n′, ARITY-SUPPORT-LIFT-001 records the increase in maximum attainable support size:


    min(k,n) → min(k,n′).


This mathal changes no arity.
It changes support capacity.


Example for k=3:
    n=1: max support 1
    n=2: max support 2
    n=3: max support 3
    n≥3: max support remains 3.


This is the mathematically clean core underneath several low-cardinality intuitions.

CHAPTER 12
OPEN PROBLEMS


12.1 Nontrivial whole-number grammars


The central classification problem remains:


Which transition types recur across mathematically unrelated families after local mechanisms are preserved and superficial index coincidence is controlled?


Problem 12.1.
Define an equivalence relation on cardinal transitions strong enough to identify genuinely shared structural mechanisms but weak enough to preserve local proofs.


12.2 Minimal mathal axioms


The current definition is intentionally permissive.


A stricter theory may require:
— source and target classes;
— partial composition;
— receipt functors;
— refusal states;
— equivalence lenses;
— support-sensitive typing.


Problem 12.2.
Find a minimal axiom system for receipt-bearing typed crossings that supports composition without collapsing endpoint, path, trace, arity, and support equivalence.


Determine exactly when this structure is already a known categorical, automata-theoretic, operadic, database-theoretic, or rewriting object.


12.3 Support-sensitive relation birth


Problem 12.3.
For a family of constrained k-ary relations, classify the possible triples


    (b_type, b_wit, b_≠).


Which triples are realizable?
Which constraints force equality among coordinates?
Which natural mathematical families exhibit strict inequalities?


12.4 Equality-pattern lattices


The equality pattern of a k-tuple is a set partition of {1,…,k}.


This suggests a lattice of collision types ordered by refinement.


Problem 12.4.
Study relation birth and obstruction across the partition lattice of coordinate equality patterns.


Questions:
— Which relation properties are monotone under collision?
— Which are monotone under de-collision?
— When does moving toward the full-distinct partition create a new obstruction?
— When does diagonal collapse simplify a relation?


12.5 Birth and obstruction duality


Relation birth and carrier obstruction remain superficially dual:
one marks first possibility;
the other marks first impossibility under a preserved target.


Problem 12.5.
Characterize families in which fine-structure birth corresponds functorially to coarse-carrier obstruction.


12.6 Receipt complexity


Problem 12.6.
Given target class Q, compute the minimum receipt sufficient to preserve every Q-relevant distinction under composition.


Special case:
How much equality-pattern information must be retained to answer future support-sensitive questions?


12.7 Decoder lattices


Decoders can be ordered by informativeness.


For tuples one natural chain is:


    exact tuple
        ⪰ equality pattern
        ⪰ support size
        ⪰ full-distinct Boolean.


Problem 12.7.
Generalize this into a preorder or lattice of decoders and study how threshold indices move under refinement.


12.8 Algorithmic discovery


Finite combinatorial databases permit searches for abrupt changes in invariants across n.


A machine may nominate thresholds.
It must not silently supply explanations.


Problem 12.8.
Design a search procedure that emits, with every candidate numathal:
— local specimen;
— adjacent controls;
— alternate decoders;
— diagonal controls;
— support spectrum;
— counterexamples;
— minimum-discriminator tests;
— and a confidence label tied to claim type rather than rhetorical strength.


12.9 Automatic nitpick generation


The Second Edition proposes a new adversarial research direction.


Problem 12.9.
Given a mathematical manuscript, generate the smallest finite counterexamples to every sentence containing words such as:
— requires;
— first;
— only;
— same;
— identical;
— participant;
— relation;
— threshold;
— must;
— impossible.


The machine should attempt:
— diagonal substitution;
— repeated-coordinate substitution;
— carrier change;
— decoder coarsening;
— quotient collapse;
— boundary values;
— empty structure;
— singleton structure;
— and alternate equivalence lenses.


The ideal output is not a criticism score.
It is a repair map.


12.10 Closing reference statement


The mature form of the subject will be measured by the quality of its refusals.


It should refuse:
— to identify equal outputs with equal histories;
— to identify arity with distinct support;
— to identify coordinate roles with participants;
— to assign a cardinal transition to number alone when the world changed;
— to treat absence under one decoder as nonexistence;
— to elevate a repeated pattern above the proofs that generated it;
— and to defend an imprecise sentence when a two-symbol counterexample can improve the grammar.


What survives those refusals is small enough to compute and large enough to compose.

APPENDIX F
EXHAUSTIVE SUPPORT CALCULUS
F.1 Why an appendix this large exists
The First Edition required one sentence of clarification.
The Second Edition responds with a finite combinatorial calculus.
This is disproportionate by design.
The original ambiguity concerned whether k coordinate places imply k distinct participants. They do not. Once this distinction is exposed, however, the resulting structure is not merely defensive. It has exact enumeration, natural quotient maps, a partition-lattice interpretation, and useful probability statements.
The repair therefore earns its page count.
F.2 Exact counting of support strata
Let X be a finite carrier with |X|=n.
Fix tuple arity k≥1.
Recall:
    S_{k,j}(X)={x∈X^k : s(x)=j}.
Theorem F.1 (Support-stratum count).
For 1≤j≤min(k,n),
    |S_{k,j}(X)| = (n)_j · {k \brace j},
where
    (n)_j = n(n−1)…(n−j+1)
is the falling factorial and
    {k \brace j}
is the Stirling number of the second kind.
Proof.
A k-tuple with exactly j distinct coordinate values determines:
1. a partition of the k coordinate positions into j nonempty equality blocks;
2. an injective assignment of j distinct carrier values to those blocks.
There are {k \brace j} partitions of the coordinate positions into j nonempty unlabeled blocks.
There are (n)_j injective assignments of j distinct carrier values to those blocks.
Multiplying yields the result. ∎
This theorem makes the arity/support distinction quantitative.
F.3 Recovery of the total tuple count
Corollary F.2.
    n^k = Σ_{j=1}^{min(k,n)} (n)_j {k \brace j}.
Proof.
The support strata partition X^k. ∎
This familiar Stirling identity now has a direct nuMathelological reading:
    TOTAL TUPLE WORLD
      =
    SUM OF DISTINCT-SUPPORT WORLDS.
A tuple space can therefore be decomposed exactly by participant multiplicity without altering arity.
F.4 The binary atlas
Let k=2.
For n≥1:
Support 1:
    |S_{2,1}| = n.
These are exactly the diagonal tuples:
    (a,a).
Support 2:
    |S_{2,2}| = n(n−1).
These are the off-diagonal tuples:
    (a,b), a≠b.
Total:
    n + n(n−1)=n².
At n=1:
    support profile = (1).
At n=2:
    support profile = (2,2).
At n=3:
    support profile = (3,6).
At n=4:
    support profile = (4,12).
The 1→2 transition therefore changes binary support capacity from:
    {1}
to
    {1,2}.
It does not change binary arity.
F.5 The ternary atlas
Let k=3.
The Stirling numbers are:
    {3 \brace 1}=1
    {3 \brace 2}=3
    {3 \brace 3}=1.
Therefore:
Support 1:
    |S_{3,1}| = n.
Support 2:
    |S_{3,2}| = 3n(n−1).
Support 3:
    |S_{3,3}| = n(n−1)(n−2).
Total:
    n + 3n(n−1) + n(n−1)(n−2) = n³.
At n=1:
    (1).
At n=2:
    (2,6).
Total 8.
At n=3:
    (3,18,6).
Total 27.
At n=4:
    (4,36,24).
Total 64.
Wolfram independently reproduced these values.
The 2→3 transition therefore adds exactly six full-distinct ternary tuples when moving from a labeled two-element carrier to a labeled three-element carrier.
Those six tuples are the 3! orderings of the three distinct labels.
This is a much sharper statement than “three-way relation begins at three.”
F.6 The quaternary atlas
For k=4:
    {4 \brace 1}=1
    {4 \brace 2}=7
    {4 \brace 3}=6
    {4 \brace 4}=1.
Thus:
    |S_{4,1}| = n
    |S_{4,2}| = 7n(n−1)
    |S_{4,3}| = 6n(n−1)(n−2)
    |S_{4,4}| = n(n−1)(n−2)(n−3).

F.8 Support polynomial
Definition F.3 (Support polynomial).
For carrier size n and arity k define
    P_{n,k}(z)
      =
    Σ_{j=1}^{min(k,n)}
      (n)_j {k \brace j} z^j.
The coefficient of z^j counts tuples with exactly j distinct participants.
Immediate checks:
    P_{n,k}(1)=n^k.
For k=2:
    P_{n,2}(z)
      =
    nz + n(n−1)z².
For k=3:
    P_{n,3}(z)
      =
    nz
      + 3n(n−1)z²
      + n(n−1)(n−2)z³.
The support polynomial is not required by the core theory.
It is useful because it packages the collision profile of the entire tuple world.
F.9 Random-tuple support
Choose a tuple uniformly from X^k.
Then:
    Pr[s(x)=j]
      =
    (n)_j {k \brace j} / n^k.
In particular, if n≥k:
    Pr[full-distinct]
      =
    (n)_k / n^k.
The probability of full diagonal is:
    Pr[s(x)=1]
      =
    n / n^k
      =
    n^{1−k}.
Thus repeated participants are not exceptional artifacts of notation. Their prevalence depends systematically on the ratio between carrier size and arity.
F.10 Collision probability
For k=2:
    Pr[x₁=x₂] = 1/n.
    Pr[x₁≠x₂] = (n−1)/n.
For larger k, the probability of all coordinates being distinct is:
    (n)_k / n^k
when n≥k, and zero when n<k.
Therefore:
    Pr[at least one collision]
      =
    1 − (n)_k/n^k
for n≥k, and 1 for n<k.
This is the classical birthday-collision shape appearing inside the relation-birthday correction, an accident of terminology that Lemon Press declines to interpret metaphysically.
F.11 Equality-pattern count
The number of possible equality patterns among k coordinate positions is the Bell number B_k.
The number with exactly j distinct support blocks is the Stirling number:
    {k \brace j}.
Thus there are three nested counting levels:
1. exact labeled tuples:
       n^k;
2. equality patterns:
       B_k;
3. support sizes:
       at most min(k,n) values.
Each is a quotient of the richer level above.
Exact tuple
    ↓ forget carrier labels
Equality partition
    ↓ forget partition shape
Number of blocks
    =
Support size.
A claim valid at the bottom cannot generally be lifted back to the top.
F.12 Coordinate permutation action
The symmetric group S_k acts on X^k by permuting coordinate positions.
This action preserves support size.
It need not preserve:
— orientation;
— role semantics;
— asymmetric relation membership;
— path receipts.
For a full-distinct tuple, the coordinate-permutation orbit has size k! when all positions are free and the tuple labels are distinct.
For tuples with repeated coordinates, stabilizers become nontrivial and orbit sizes shrink.
Thus repeated support changes symmetry as well as participant count.
F.13 Relation support profile
For arbitrary relation R⊆X^k, define the count vector:
    c_j(R)
      =
    |R ∩ S_{k,j}(X)|.
Then:
    |R|
      =
    Σ_j c_j(R).
Definition F.4 (Support profile).
The support profile of R is
    C(R)=(c_1,…,c_min(k,n)).
The support spectrum Σ(R) records only which c_j are nonzero.
The support profile records multiplicity as well.
Examples.
Equality relation on X²:
    C(R)=(n,0).
Disequality relation on X²:
    C(R)=(0,n(n−1)).
Universal binary relation X²:
    C(R)=(n,n(n−1)).
A graph with loops permitted has both support-1 and support-2 edge possibilities.
A simple loopless graph occupies only the support-2 stratum.
This single distinction resolves a large family of informal graph/relation confusions.
F.14 Support-conditioned relations
Definition F.5.
A relation R⊆X^k is j-supported when every tuple in R has support size j.
Equality on X² is 1-supported.
Disequality on X² is 2-supported.
A nondegenerate oriented triangle relation may be 3-supported.
A relation is support-homogeneous when it is j-supported for some j.
Otherwise it is support-mixed.
Support homogeneity is independent of arity.
F.15 Distinctness constraints as predicates
Rather than treating distinctness as hidden prose, encode it.
Define:
    δ_k(x₁,…,x_k)
      ⇔
    ∀i≠j, x_i≠x_j.
Then a “k-way all-distinct relation” is more accurately represented as:
    R(x₁,…,x_k) ∧ δ_k(x₁,…,x_k).
The carrier threshold n≥k comes from δ_k, not from arity k alone.
This factorization should be preferred whenever the distinction matters.
F.16 Weak and strong relation birth
Definition F.6 (Weak birth).

Definition F.7 (Strong support-j birth).

For unconstrained X^k:
    weak birth = 1
for every k≥1 over nonempty carriers.

    j.

F.18 Support monotonicity
A relation family may or may not be monotone under carrier enlargement.
For the universal relation, support availability is monotone.
For a constrained relation defined using arithmetic or geometry, enlarging the carrier may introduce or destroy properties depending on how the family itself changes.
Therefore:
    support capacity monotonicity
    ≠
    relation property monotonicity.
The generic theorem controls only what tuples are available, not which ones the relation selects.
F.19 The exact low-number theorem
Theorem F.9.
For fixed k and universal tuple worlds X_n^k with |X_n|=n, the transition n−1→n for 1<n≤k introduces the first tuples of support size n and introduces exactly
    (n)_n {k \brace n}
      =
    n! {k \brace n}
such tuples when the carrier grows from n−1 to n and the labels of the n-element world are fixed.
Care is required:
the entire tuple space also changes because old support strata gain additional labeled tuples involving the new carrier element.
Thus “new stratum appears” is not the same as “only new tuples lie in the new stratum.”
The Second Edition records both facts.
F.20 Old-stratum growth under carrier enlargement
Moving from n−1 to n increases the count in every support-j stratum with 1≤j≤min(k,n−1):
    Δ_j
      =
    [(n)_j − (n−1)_j] {k \brace j}.
So cardinal enlargement has two simultaneous effects:
1. existing support strata gain new labeled realizations;
2. when n≤k, a previously impossible support-n stratum appears.
The second is qualitative support birth.
The first is quantitative expansion.
This is another non-collapse:
    NEW STRATUM ≠ ONLY NEW CONTENT.
